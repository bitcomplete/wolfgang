# Research survey: Multi-agent SDLC orchestration (2024 – July 2026)

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Purpose:** Inform the software-garden July 2026 release spec (first user: a junior engineer operating runtime for 4–5 apps). Survey of arXiv (cs.SE / cs.MA / cs.AI) and adjacent industry sources on multi-agent SDLC orchestration.
**Method:** Web search (multiple angles) + fetching key papers' abstracts/HTML. Summaries of fetched papers were produced by an automated reader over the page content; percentages and claims below were taken from those abstracts/pages, not from full-paper reading — flagged where evidence quality is uncertain.

---

## 1. The single-agent vs. multi-agent debate — where it landed by mid-2026

### 1.1 Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail? (MAST)
- **Cite:** Cemri, Pan, Yang et al., arXiv:2503.13657 (v3 Oct 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657
- **Core claim:** Introduces MAST, a taxonomy of **14 failure modes in 3 categories** — (i) system design / specification issues, (ii) inter-agent misalignment, (iii) task verification — from **1,600+ annotated traces across 7 MAS frameworks** (κ = 0.88 inter-annotator agreement). Performance gains of MAS over single agents on popular benchmarks are "often minimal"; failures stem from *system design*, not just model limits, and "require more than superficial fixes."
- **Evidence quality:** High — large annotated dataset, ICML-track paper, widely cited; the canonical failure taxonomy for this space.
- **Garden implication:** Greenwood's design reviews should literally use MAST's three buckets as a checklist. Notably, "task verification" being a top-level failure category validates keeping verifier design (and the KB's open verifier-of-the-verifier question) on the critical path, and "specification issues" argues for structured task contracts on the bus rather than free-text handoffs.

### 1.2 The June-2025 industry fork: Cognition vs. Anthropic
- **Cites:** Cognition, "Don't Build Multi-Agents" (June 12, 2025); Anthropic, "How we built our multi-agent research system" (June 13, 2025). Secondary coverage: https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-anthropic-built-a-multi-agent , https://llmmultiagents.com/en/blogs/anthropic-multi-agent-system-reflection
- **Core claims:** Cognition: parallel subagents make independent decisions on shared problems → conflicting outputs; prefer one agent with full context. Anthropic: orchestrator + 3–5 parallel subagents works for breadth-first research; **token usage explains ~80% of performance variance**, and multi-agent "works mainly because it helps spend enough tokens" — at **~15× the token cost** of a chat interaction.
- **Evidence quality:** Medium (engineering blogs, not peer-reviewed), but both are from teams running production systems and the positions have held up.
- **Garden implication:** Multi-agent is justified for *parallelizable, breadth-first* work, not as a default. For the July release: default to a single strong agent per app-ops task; reserve multi-agent fan-out for read-only investigation (log/metric triage across 4–5 apps is exactly breadth-first). Budget/cost controls are first-class, not M6 polish — 15× token multipliers will hit a bootstrapped consultancy's wallet fast.

### 1.3 SWE-bench evidence: scaffolding matters, but adaptively
- **Agyn** (Benkovich & Valkov, arXiv:2602.01465, Feb 2026 — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01465v2): team-structured MAS (coordination / research / implementation / review roles, isolated sandboxes, structured communication, PR + iterative review methodology) reports **72.2% on SWE-bench-500**, beating single-agent baselines on comparable models; claims it was built for production, not benchmark-tuned. Evidence quality: medium (self-reported, small author team).
- **icat-agent** (arXiv:2606.25514 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25514): decentralized MAS using **synchronous event-based message passing instead of shared context**; *adaptive* workflow pivoting (parallel patch+validate for well-specified issues, exploration first for poorly-specified ones, gated by rubric-based quality checks). Beats SWE-agent/mini-SWE-agent/Claude Code by 3.6–8.4% on SWE-bench Verified and 6.3–18.5% on SWE-bench Pro, **while reducing cost ~$1.18/instance** vs a multi-agent Claude Code baseline. Evidence quality: medium-high (standard benchmarks, cost reported).
- **Counter-signal:** SWE-agent 1.0-style single agents remained SOTA-competitive into 2026; several analyses argue the bottleneck is the model, not scaffolding (e.g. https://www.programming-helper.com/tech/swe-bench-coding-agent-benchmarks-2026-software-engineering-ai-evaluation — low evidence quality, blog).
- **Garden implication:** The winning 2026 pattern is **conditional orchestration**: cheap classifier/rubric decides whether a task gets a single agent or a team. This matches Wolfgang's D7 (bus-side decomposition) — decomposition should be a *bus decision informed by task quality*, not a fixed pipeline. Message-passing-instead-of-shared-context (icat-agent) directly supports D8 (messaging as the only inter-agent primitive).

### 1.4 Asynchronous coordination: CAID
- **Cite:** Geng & Neubig, "Effective Strategies for Asynchronous Software Engineering Agents," arXiv:2603.21489 (Mar 2026, rev. July 8, 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21489
- **Core claim:** **Centralized Asynchronous Isolated Delegation (CAID)** — a manager centrally delegates; workers run asynchronously in **isolated git worktrees**; integration happens through **branch-and-merge with test-based verification**. Gains: +25.6% absolute on PaperBench reproduction, +14.7% on Commit0 library development. Identifies branch-and-merge as *the* central coordination mechanism, i.e., reuse boring SE primitives rather than inventing agent-native ones.
- **Evidence quality:** High for the benchmarks used (credible authors; note the summary I fetched attributed affiliation to Anthropic — Neubig is CMU, so treat affiliation as unverified).
- **Garden implication:** Strong precedent for the garden's temporary-bridges principle: coordinate agents through git/tests/PRs the junior operator already understands, not bespoke shared state. For Greenwood, "isolated workspace + merge gate" is the unit of parallelism; the event log records delegations and merges.

## 2. Orchestration architecture patterns

### 2.1 Planner–executor–verifier as the consensus production shape
- **Cites:** PEAR (planner-executor robustness benchmark, arXiv:2510.07505 — https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07505v3); "Architecting Resilient LLM Agents: secure plan-then-execute" (arXiv:2509.08646 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08646); Routine (enterprise structural planning, arXiv:2507.14447 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.14447); survey of code-gen agents (arXiv:2508.00083 — https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00083v1).
- **Core claim (converging across papers):** best practice is to **separate planning from execution**, with the planner emitting explicit constraints and success criteria and the executor running under *stricter tool permissions*; a verifier (or verification pass) closes the loop. Production systems (e.g. Manus) ship exactly this three-stage pipeline for traceability.
- **Evidence quality:** Medium-high in aggregate — many independent sources converge; individual papers vary.
- **Garden implication:** Per-role least-privilege (executor has narrower permissions than planner) is cheap to enforce when all actions flow through the bus (D8 per-message governance). For a junior operator, "the plan is a reviewable artifact with success criteria" is also the natural human approval gate — approve plans, not diffs.

### 2.2 Orchestration surveys
- **"The Orchestration of Multi-Agent Systems: Architectures, Protocols, and Enterprise Adoption"** (Adimulam, Gupta, Kumar, arXiv:2601.13671, Jan 2026 — https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13671v1): three-layer framework (worker/service/support agents; orchestration layer = planning/policy, execution/control, state/knowledge, quality/ops management; protocol layer = MCP + A2A). Lands on **"supervised peer coordination"**: agents may talk peer-to-peer but a control unit supervises for policy alignment — neither pure hub-and-spoke nor a free-for-all. Enterprise numbers cited (95%+ underwriting accuracy, 20× faster approvals, "80% of support incidents") are vendor-case-study grade — treat as **low evidence quality**.
- **MDPI Future Internet survey of frameworks/protocols/patterns** (lit cutoff Mar 2026 — https://doi.org/10.3390/fi18060326): compares LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen/MAF, OpenAI Agents SDK, MetaGPT, DSPy on state-management granularity, token-cost structure, failure recovery, design philosophy. Useful comparison axes; no strong original results.
- **Workflow-graph line of work:** "From Static Templates to Dynamic Runtime Graphs" (arXiv:2603.22386 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22386), HALO (arXiv:2505.13516), "Multi-Agent Collaboration via Evolving Orchestration" (arXiv:2505.19591), RL over orchestration traces (arXiv:2605.02801 — https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02801v1). Direction of travel: workflows move from hand-authored templates to graphs optimized/adapted at runtime. Evidence quality: research-stage; not production-proven.
- **Garden implication:** "Supervised peer coordination" is precisely Greenwood's shape (agents message freely; the bus applies per-message governance). The framework-comparison axes (state granularity, token-cost structure, failure recovery) are good column headers for any Wolfgang build-vs-adopt decision memo. Dynamic runtime graphs are post-July research, not release material.

### 2.3 Agentic SE as a discipline: SASE / SE 3.0
- **"Agentic Software Engineering: Foundational Pillars and a Research Roadmap"** (Hassan, Li, Lin, Adams, Chen, Kashiwa, Qiu — arXiv:2509.06216, Sept 2025, rev. June 24, 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06216): the SASE vision. Duality of **SE-for-Humans / SE-for-Agents**; two workbenches — **ACE (Agent Command Environment)** where a human supervises and mentors agent teams, consuming **Merge-Readiness Packs** and **Consultation Request Packs**, and **AEE (Agent Execution Environment)** where agents work and *initiate* human callbacks/handovers. Evidence quality: position paper (no empirical results) but from the most-cited SE-research group in this area; it names the artifacts everyone else is converging on.
- **"The Rise of AI Teammates in SE 3.0" + AIDev dataset** (Li, Zhang, Hassan, arXiv:2507.15003, July 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15003): **456k agent-authored PRs, 61k repos, 47k developers, 5 agents (Codex, Devin, Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code)**. Findings: agent PRs are fast but have **lower acceptance rates than human PRs** and are structurally simpler; the binding constraint is the **human review bottleneck** and a trust gap. Evidence quality: high (large-N mining study, dataset public).
- **Garden implication:** The July release's operator surface *is* an ACE in miniature. Adopt the "Merge-Readiness Pack" idea: every agent action ships as a reviewable bundle (what/why/evidence/tests/rollback plan) so a junior can adjudicate without reconstructing context. AIDev quantifies the risk of shipping agent throughput without matching review tooling — throughput without adjudication tooling just moves the queue to the human.

## 3. Error cascades and verification placement (Greenwood's theoretical footing)

### 3.1 From Spark to Fire — the KB's grounding paper, verified
- **Cite:** Xie, Zhu, Zhang, Zhu, Ye, Qi, Chen, Zhou — arXiv:2603.04474 (Mar 2026, rev. May 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04474
- **Core claim:** Models MAS collaboration as a **directed dependency graph** with an early-stage amplification-risk criterion; three vulnerability classes — **cascade amplification, topological sensitivity, consensus inertia**. Mitigation: a **genealogy-graph-based governance layer implemented as a message-layer plugin** (no changes to the collaboration architecture) that **prevents final infection in ≥89% of runs**.
- **Evidence quality:** Medium-high — simulation + experiments per abstract; recent, citation record still thin. Verified: this paper exists and says what the Wolfgang KB claims it says.
- **Garden implication:** Direct validation of Greenwood's two core bets: (a) genealogy/lineage of messages as first-class data, (b) governance enforced *at the message layer* (D8). The "89% infection prevention" number is the headline stat to cite in the release spec's rationale section. The "consensus inertia" class is a new input to the verifier-of-the-verifier open question: once agents agree, a late verifier struggles — verify early.

### 3.2 Verification timing and placement
- **"Delayed Verification Destabilizes Multi-Agent LLM Belief"** (arXiv:2606.27409, June 2026 — https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27409v1): verification has a **stability budget** — corrections that are too strong *or too late* turn consensus into oscillation. Closed-form thresholds (max safe correction strength falls with verification latency; ≈0.618 ceiling at delay 2); **place correctors on amplifier/bridge nodes** (greedy 1−1/e-optimal); **grounded (evidence-anchored) verification stabilizes even under delay, ungrounded criticism destabilizes**. LLM experiments match predictions in 96–100% of predicted-unstable conditions. Evidence quality: medium-high — strong theory, modest-scale LLM validation (n=8 question-level).
- **Hallucination Cascade** (Jamshidi et al., arXiv:2606.07937 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07937): 500 cascade experiments, 3 heterogeneous models; hallucination in MAS is a *dynamic* process shaped by interaction history, cascade depth, model heterogeneity. Companion: "Collective hallucination" (arXiv:2606.07941). **PropGuard** (Yan et al., arXiv:2605.16346): propagation-aware exploration + remediation.
- **Garden implication:** Three actionable rules for Greenwood: (1) verifiers must be **evidence-grounded** (tests, logs, metrics — never "another LLM opining"); (2) verify **early and at topological hotspots** (the bus knows the dependency graph, so it can compute where); (3) *more* verification is not monotonically better — this reframes verifier-of-the-verifier from "add another layer" to "tune dose and placement," and the event-sourced log is exactly the data needed to do that.

## 4. Agent communication protocols and their governance gaps

- **Protocol survey:** "A Survey of Agent Interoperability Protocols: MCP, ACP, A2A, ANP" (arXiv:2505.02279, May 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02279). MCP = JSON-RPC tool/context access; A2A = peer agent collaboration (agent cards, tasks); ACP = REST/MIME multipart async messaging; ANP = open-network discovery via DIDs. Evidence quality: high as a descriptive survey; the field's standard reference. A2A/MCP have clearly won mindshare through 2026 (secondary: https://zylos.ai/research/2026-03-26-agent-interoperability-protocols-mcp-a2a-acp-convergence/ — blog, low evidence quality).
- **Governance gaps:** Kang & Diponegoro, "Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols: What MCP, A2A, and ACP Cannot Express" (arXiv:2606.31498, July 2026 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31498). **CORRECTION (2026-07-29, full-text check — `research-round2-stat-verification.md` item 11):** the paper's actual six-dimension taxonomy is membership, deliberation, voting, dissent preservation, human escalation, and audit/replay. It does **not** analyze budget/rate-limit or delegation-constraint expressiveness — that gap list was an automated-reader artifact of round 1. Budgets/delegation gaps remain supported by the orchestration survey and industry complaints; cite those for that point, and cite this paper only for audit/replay, escalation, and deliberation/dissent gaps.
- **Semantic critique:** "Beyond Message Passing: A Semantic View of Agent Communication Protocols" (arXiv:2604.02369) — message-passing standards don't guarantee shared meaning. Noted, not deeply reviewed.
- **Garden implication:** This is the strongest external validation of Wolfgang's D8 decision found in this survey: per-message governance (budgets, delegation constraints, audit) is exactly what the standard protocols *cannot* do, so Greenwood putting governance in the bus is differentiation, not NIH. Practical release note: speak MCP at the tool edge for interop, but keep governance semantics in Greenwood's own message envelope (correlation_id/context_ref lineage à la Chattermax's 12-message taxonomy).

## 5. Human oversight of agent fleets

- **The attention-scaling problem:** machine-initiated work scales faster than human sensemaking; supervisors of agent fleets drown in approvals/alerts/exceptions (Mistretta, "Human Oversight Under Load," Mar 2026 — https://medium.com/@maxdolphin/human-oversight-under-load-in-the-age-of-ai-agents-e943b6e6720d — low evidence quality, essay; but consistent with AIDev's measured review bottleneck).
- **Anthropic 2026 agentic-coding trends** (https://claude.com/blog/eight-trends-defining-how-software-gets-built-in-2026 — vendor report, medium-low evidence quality): 2026 = shift from single assistants to **agent teams running hours/days**; priority areas include **scaling human oversight via AI-automated review** — agents self-flag uncertainty, AI reviewers check other agents, humans get only escalations (layered quality control).
- **Graduated oversight:** Kang, "Governed AI-Assisted Engineering: Graduated Human Oversight for Agentic Code Generation in Regulated Domains" (arXiv:2606.22484 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22484): tier review intensity by **risk classification × agent maturity × output criticality**; establish baseline agent performance before relaxing review (progressive trust). Evidence quality: medium (framework paper, limited empirics).
- **Autonomy measurement:** "Measuring AI agent autonomy via code inspection" (arXiv:2502.15212) — autonomy levels can be assessed statically from orchestration code; useful for trust-tier assignment.
- **Garden implication:** Converges on the garden's existing doctrine (risk tiering, patrols, middle loop, mahdi's agent trust profiles) — the literature now backs it. For the release: (1) risk-tiered approval gates are MVP (auto-execute low-risk, gate high-risk); (2) **progressive trust must be legible to a junior** — trust tiers should come from recorded track record in the event log, not operator vibes; (3) an AI-review layer between agents and the human is how one junior supervises 4–5 apps without drowning.

### 5.1 Junior engineers specifically
- **"From Junior to Senior: Allocating Agency in Agentic-AI-Mediated SE"** (arXiv:2602.00496, Feb 2026 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00496): interview/observation study. Juniors **accept agent output more readily, sometimes without comprehension**; seniors retain interrogative oversight and final authority. Recommends explicit protocols for when humans hold decision authority, review-as-learning, tools designed to promote understanding, and mentorship to contextualize AI output. Evidence quality: medium-high (qualitative empirical).
- **Anthropic skill-formation study** (52 engineers, mostly junior — https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills ; arXiv:2601.20245): AI assistance **impairs conceptual understanding and debugging when used passively**; strongest learners used AI for conceptual follow-ups, not just generation. Trust context: only ~29% of developers trust AI output accuracy, down from 40% in 2024 (Sonar State of Code 2026 — https://www.sonarsource.com/state-of-code-developer-survey-report.pdf).
- **Garden implication:** The release's junior operator is *predicted by the literature* to over-approve. Countermeasures belong in the spec: (a) approval UIs must show evidence (test results, metric deltas, diffs), not just "agent recommends X — approve?"; (b) require a stated reason on approvals of high-risk actions; (c) default-deny for irreversible ops regardless of operator confidence; (d) explanations-with-actions doubles as the mentorship channel the study says juniors lose.

## 6. Agents in operations / AIOps-SRE (the release's actual domain)

- **AIOpsLab** (Microsoft, arXiv:2501.06706; FSE 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06706 , https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3696630.3728619): holistic framework — deploys microservice environments, injects faults, generates load, exports telemetry, and orchestrates agent evaluation across detection → localization → RCA → mitigation. Evidence quality: high (open platform, Microsoft Research, peer-reviewed).
- **SREGym** (arXiv:2605.07161, May 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07161 ; https://github.com/SREGym/SREGym): live benchmark, **90 realistic SRE problems** incl. metastable and correlated failures, ambient noise; supersets AIOpsLab + ITBench problems. **Frontier agents: 38.9–72.6% diagnosis success, 57.3–78.5% mitigation success**, with up to 40-point spread across failure kinds. Evidence quality: high.
- **Multi-dataset microservice failure-diagnosis benchmark** (arXiv:2606.29193, June 2026 — https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29193v1): consistent theme — agent diagnosis quality is highly dataset/failure-type dependent.
- **Deterministic multi-agent incident response** (arXiv:2511.15755, Nov 2025, v2 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15755): 348 controlled trials; multi-agent orchestration gives **zero quality variance across trials** (vs inconsistent single-agent output), 100% actionable-recommendation rate vs 1.7% single-agent, similar ~40s latency. Evidence quality: **medium-low** — single unreplicated study, eye-popping ratios, authors themselves expanded limitations in v2. Treat the *consistency* finding as the credible part: structure reduces variance, which is what SLAs need.
- **Flow-of-Action** (2025): SOP-grounded multi-agent RCA — encode runbooks/SOPs as structure for the agents. **IPCopilot**: 4 agents in inference-action-reflection loops mimicking human IR teams. **UModel** (2026): observability data modeled *for agent consumption*. (Index: https://github.com/Jun-jie-Huang/awesome-LLM-AIOps)
- **AIR: Improving Agent Safety through Incident Response** (arXiv:2602.11749 — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11749v2): applies IR discipline *to the agents themselves* — incident playbooks for when agents misbehave.
- **Garden implication:** (1) Current agents mitigate better than they diagnose deep/novel failures — so the release should ship agents as **diagnosis assistants + gated mitigation executors**, not autonomous healers; the 38.9–72.6% diagnosis range is the quantitative argument for approval gates. (2) SOP-grounding (Flow-of-Action) maps directly onto Chattermax/Trellis runbook templates: the junior's runbooks become agent SOPs — one artifact, two consumers. (3) Adopt an SREGym-style fault-injection drill as pre-release validation for the ops agents on bc-prod-like staging. (4) Plan an "agent incident" playbook (AIR): what the junior does when an *agent* is the incident.

## 7. Long-running agent runtime durability (industry practice, 2026)

- **Cites (all industry-blog grade, low-medium evidence quality, but unanimous):** https://slavadubrov.github.io/blog/2026/05/26/ai-agent-runtime/ , https://appscale.blog/en/blog/durable-execution-llm-agents-temporal-langgraph-checkpointing-2026 , https://www.indium.tech/blog/7-state-persistence-strategies-ai-agents-2026/
- **Consensus stack for agents running >hours:** durable state *outside the model* — session logs, checkpoints after each major step, heartbeats with progress data, resume-from-checkpoint on crash, sandbox isolation, policy checks, secret brokering, **cost limits**. "Queue + worker + checkpoint DB is a strong owned-stack default"; durable-execution engines (Temporal, Restate, DBOS, Inngest) or LangGraph checkpointers are the buy options. One cited claim: >4h agents without persistence carry ~90% higher total-failure risk (unverified vendor number).
- **Garden implication:** Greenwood's event-sourced log **is** the checkpoint/heartbeat substrate — fold-the-log restore is architecturally ahead of the industry's bolt-on checkpointing. But the release spec must make **resume/rollback an operator-facing button**, not an architectural property: the junior's most common task will be "this agent died at minute 8 — resume it." Chattermax's memory-only freeze state is the anti-pattern to explicitly avoid.

## 8. Synthesis: what the corpus says the July release should do

1. **Conditional, not maximal, multi-agent.** Single strong agent by default; fan out only for breadth-first work (multi-app triage) or well-specified parallelizable tasks, gated by a cheap task-quality rubric (icat-agent, Anthropic/Cognition, MAST).
2. **Coordinate via boring SE primitives.** Isolated workspaces + branch-and-merge + test-verified integration (CAID); plans as reviewable artifacts with success criteria (plan-then-execute); Merge-Readiness-Pack-style bundles for every human gate (SASE).
3. **Governance lives in the bus.** Per-message budgets, delegation constraints, audit, lineage — gaps the protocols don't fill (audit/replay + escalation per 2606.31498, corrected 2026-07-29; budgets/delegation per the orchestration survey and industry reports) and the message-layer genealogy governance Spark-to-Fire showed blocks ≥89% of cascades. D8 is well-founded; keep MCP only at the tool edge.
4. **Verification: grounded, early, placed — not maximal.** Evidence-anchored verifiers at topological hotspots; beware delayed/ungrounded correction causing oscillation (2606.27409). Reframes the verifier-of-the-verifier open question as dose-and-placement tuning over the event log.
5. **Ops agents propose, gates dispose.** With frontier diagnosis at 38.9–72.6% on realistic SRE faults (SREGym), autonomous mitigation is unjustified; risk-tiered approval gates with progressive, track-record-based trust are MVP (graduated-oversight literature + garden's own risk-tiering doctrine).
6. **Design the approval surface for a junior who will over-trust.** Evidence-rich approval cards, default-deny on irreversible actions, reasons required on high-risk approvals, AI-review layer to compress the queue (2602.00496, Anthropic skill-formation, AIDev review bottleneck).
7. **Resume/rollback and cost caps are operator features, not milestones M3/M6.** The Wolfgang KB currently schedules all operator-facing work last; every strand of this survey (oversight-under-load, durability practice, 15× token costs, junior-trust findings) says that ordering is backwards for a July ship with this user.

## 9. Gaps and cautions in this survey
- Several key numbers (89% cascade prevention, 72.2% Agyn, SREGym ranges) come from abstracts via an automated reader; verify against full PDFs before quoting in external-facing docs.
- arXiv:2511.15755 (deterministic incident response) is an outlier study with extreme effect sizes and no independent replication — cite only its variance-reduction framing.
- Enterprise-adoption statistics in the orchestration surveys are vendor-case-study grade.
- One affiliation claim (CAID authors at Anthropic) could not be verified and Neubig is publicly at CMU — flagged as uncertain.
- Not covered for depth reasons: RL-trained orchestrators (2605.02801), semantic-protocol critiques (2604.02369), PEAR robustness details, SWE-EVO/ChainSWE long-horizon benchmarks — none appeared release-critical, all are tracked by URL above.
